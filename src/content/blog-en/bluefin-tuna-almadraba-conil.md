If there's one word that sums up Conil for those who come to eat — and there are plenty who come just to eat — that word is **almadraba**.

The first time a guest asked me *"What's this almadraba thing, a festival?"* I realized it wasn't obvious to those arriving from outside. So let's go step by step, the way they explained it to me when I arrived.

## What is the almadraba (and why here and not elsewhere)

The almadraba is a tuna fishing technique that's over **three thousand years old**: the Phoenicians already practiced it on these coasts. It consists of installing a labyrinth of fixed nets in the migration route of bluefin tuna as they swim up from the Atlantic to the Mediterranean to spawn. The tuna enter, and the final chamber — the *copo* — closes at the right moment.

It's not aquaculture or trawling: every piece that comes from an almadraba was selected by hand, right there, the same way it's been done for three millennia. That's where that dense, almost whole-sea flavor comes from, which bears absolutely no resemblance to supermarket tuna.

Conil is part of the small Andalusian club that keeps this tradition alive — along with Barbate and Zahara — and here tuna isn't just a menu item: it's part of the town's calendar. There are Conileño families with entire generations of almadraba fishermen behind them.

## When to come: the season

The main almadraba campaign runs approximately **from March to June**, following the bluefin tuna migration. The peak months are April, May and June, and around it revolves the famous **Tuna Route** of the region: weeks when bars and restaurants compete with tapas and dishes centered on tuna.

My neighborly advice: if you come in May, ask at any bar what their Route proposal is. It's the perfect excuse to do a tapas crawl with a common theme.

## The tuna map: what part to order

Almadraba tuna is broken down almost completely — here we call it *zero waste*: every corner of the fish ends up in a different dish. The cuts you'll see on the menu:

- **Ventresca** — the fattiest, silkiest part. If it's your first time, start here.
- **Morrillo** — the nape area, juicy and full of character. In Conil it's grilled or served with onions.
- **Tataki** — seared on the outside, raw inside. The way to confirm that tuna here is on another level.
- **Atún encebollado** — slow, home-style stew, the kind mothers and grandmothers make. Truly comforting.
- **Tuna fritters** or **ortiguillas** (sea anemones) on the side — pure Cádiz frying.

And one thing I learned as a kid: **don't add salt or lemon without tasting first**. Good almadraba tuna is already seasoned by the sea itself.

## Where I eat it

I'm not going to give you a generic top-10 list; I'll give you my personal map, by situation:

- **For midday tapas:** any of the bars in the Mercado de Abastos square. Order a tapa of tuna with onions, a beer, and you're touching the gastronomic heart of the town.
- **To sit down and enjoy:** the old town restaurants have serious tuna menus all year round; during Tuna Route season they add special proposals. Book in August, always.
- **To buy and cook it yourself:** the Mercado de Abastos. The fishmonger will cut you the piece you need and explain how to cook it. If you're staying in our apartment, you have a fully equipped kitchen and the best fish on the Costa de la Luz just five minutes away.

## How I cook it at home

The simplest and most spectacular recipe: **homemade tataki in ten minutes**.

1. Buy a clean bluefin tuna loin (ask the fishmonger for a whole loin, not fillets).
2. Sear it for thirty seconds per side in a very hot pan, with no oil or just a drop.
3. Wrap it in cling film and into the freezer for twenty minutes.
4. Slice it thinly with a very sharp knife.
5. Sauce: soy sauce, a drizzle of sesame oil, grated ginger… or, Conil-style version: good olive oil, flaky salt and a touch of sherry vinegar.

With bread and a tomato salad from the market, that's a ten-out-of-ten dinner. And if you come in May, make it the same day you buy it: it won't taste the same any day after.

## Take it home with you

If you leave wanting to do it all again, in the old town there are shops that sell **almadraba bluefin tuna preserves** — ventresca in oil, mojama, roe. It's the souvenir you actually enjoy: in September, you open the jar, toast some slices… and you're back in Conil for ten minutes.
