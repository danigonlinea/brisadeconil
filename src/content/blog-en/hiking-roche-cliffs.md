If you're only going to do one walk in your week in Conil, make it this: **the Roche cliff**.

It's the face of the town that almost nobody photographs. Everyone comes for the sand and stays at Los Bateles; few head north and discover that here the coast rises in ochre walls above coves of transparent water. It's a different Conil.

## The viewpoint: best views of the Conil coast

The high stretch of the cliff is a **natural viewpoint** that extends for kilometers. From above you see the coves dropping one after another — Roqueo, Camarón, Puntalejos, Cala Lee — and in the distance, on clear days, **Cape Trafalgar** with its lighthouse.

The most photogenic spot is half an hour from the old quarter: a curve in the path where the cliff falls vertically to the sea. That's where I sit to have breakfast with a thermos of coffee when someone comes to stay. It's the plan.

## The route, short version

Leave the old quarter heading north and follow the coastline: Fuente del Gallo, and from there the path that runs along the top of the cliff, with the **coves of Roche** appearing below one after another — Roqueo, Camarón, Puntalejos, Cala Lee. In the distance, on clear days, you can see **Cape Trafalgar** with its lighthouse.

In terms of time: counting stops to look (and you'll stop a lot), allow **an hour and a half to the coves area** from the old quarter, and the same back. It's mostly flat: what tires you isn't the gradient, it's the sun.

You can shorten it two ways: drive to the Roche area and walk just the cliff section, or do half and come back the way you came. There's no obligation to reach any particular spot — the scenery is just as good halfway.

## The best time (this really matters)

**First thing in the morning or the two hours before sunset.** There's no shade on the entire route, no fountains, and in July and August the midday stretch is a bad idea.

If you choose the afternoon, plan the return with light: on the cliff it gets dark sooner than it seems and some sections run close to the edge. And if you're going down to a cove, check the **tide** first: at high tide many lose almost all their sand and you're left without a place to put your towel.

## What to bring

- **Water**, more than you think. Nowhere to refill.
- **Hat and sunscreen.** The westerly wind is deceptive: it burns just as much.
- **Closed shoes.** There's sand, loose rock and wooden steps; flip-flops suffer.
- **Swimsuit under your clothes.** If the coves are calling your name at the bottom, you'll want to say yes.
- **Something to eat.** There are no bars up there, and the walk works up an appetite.

## The short version for the lazy

If the full walk seems too much: drive or take the bus to the Roche area, walk the cliff section only (forty minutes, flat), and end with a dip in **Cala Lee**. Same cliff, same coves, half the effort.
