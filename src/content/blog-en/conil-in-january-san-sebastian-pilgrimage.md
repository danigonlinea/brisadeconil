On 6 January Conil wakes up differently. The day before, the village fills up to watch the Three Kings arrive; the day after, the streets belong to the people who live here again. January is the quietest month of the year, and it also holds the town's oldest festival.

## The San Sebastián pilgrimage

San Sebastián is the patron saint of Conil. The pilgrimage that carries him to the El Colorado pine woods has been held since the 16th century and brings together more than **20,000 people** for a day in the countryside.

The date moves every year: it falls on **the Sunday closest to 20 January**. In 2026 it was Sunday 18 January. In 2027, 20 January is a Wednesday, so the pilgrimage will land on one of the Sundays around it. The council confirms it with the poster and the programme, which come out in December or in the first weeks of January.

## The day, hour by hour

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Pilgrims' mass at the Santa Catalina parish church |
| Late morning | The procession sets off: the saint's cart, riders and carts decorated with flowers and paper lanterns |
| Before midday | Stop at the Virgen de Fátima parish church for a welcome prayer |
| Midday and afternoon | A day in the El Colorado pine woods: stews, cured meats, singing and the decorated-cart contest |
| 18:00 | The saint heads back into town |

The route through the streets has not changed in years: Padre Ramírez, Prieta, San Sebastián, the El Punto roundabout and Rosa de los Vientos, then the country tracks out to El Colorado. The image carried is a carving by Pedro Daín dated 1588.

The original chapel disappeared in the 19th century and the pilgrimage was lost for decades. More than thirty years ago the neighbours brought it back, and today it fills the pine woods.

## How to join in if you are visiting

This is not a show put on for visitors: it is the town's big day, and it works best if you walk in as one more neighbour.

- **Go early.** Mass is at nine and the procession leaves soon after; by mid-morning the tracks and the access to the pine woods fill up.
- **Wear comfortable shoes and warm clothes.** It is January: a good day makes for a superb afternoon in the woods, a wet one makes for mud.
- **Eat there.** It is a day in the countryside: people share stews and buy from the stalls in the pine woods. That is part of it.
- **Follow the route.** The decorated-cart contest is run by the Fiestas department and is where the atmosphere is.
- **If you want to take part with a cart or run a bar**, applications go to the council in the first weeks of January.

## What else January is good for

January is a month for the village, not the beach. The good news is that most of what makes Conil good works better without crowds.

- **Walk the coast.** The [Roche cliffs path](/en/blog/hiking-roche-cliffs/) in January, with low light and a heavy sea, is a different walk.
- **Walk the beach.** Not for swimming, but Los Bateles and Castilnovo at low tide are a plan in themselves. The [beach guide by profile](/en/blog/conil-beaches-guide/) works in winter too.
- **The Mercado de Abastos**, right opposite the apartment, runs all year. In January the fishmonger has time to explain what is there and how to cook it.
- **Wander the old town**, climb the Torre de Guzmán and check what is on at La Chanca.
- **Eat from the pot.** Fish stews, shellfish, pulses and the winter dishes of Cádiz. It is the best time of year to eat in Conil without booking.

## The weather, plainly

January is the coldest month of the year on the Costa de la Luz, and it is worth knowing that before you book: mild days when the sun is out, cold nights and a real chance of rain. Bring a warm jacket, closed shoes and a small umbrella.

What you do get is clean light and empty beaches. Good for walking, for reading in a bar with the door open and for seeing the village without its August layer.

## Where to check the pilgrimage

The poster and programme are published by the Fiestas department of the [Conil council](https://www.conildelafrontera.es/areas-y-servicios-municipales/fiestas) and announced on its Instagram, [@ayto_conil](https://www.instagram.com/ayto_conil/), and on the [Fiestas Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/delegaciondefiestas.deconil/).

Two practical warnings: the council has no official TikTok account, and the tourism portal's events calendar is not always up to date. If you are coming for the pilgrimage, confirm the date on the council website before you plan the trip.

## The practical side of January

Low season at its purest: you park wherever you like, prices are the lowest of the year and the bars in the old town are full of locals, not tourists. The other side is part of the deal too: beach bars are closed, some restaurants take January as holiday and shops shorten their hours.
