Some guests ask me about restaurants. And some guests ask me about the market. To the second group I tell the truth: **they end up eating better and cheaper than anyone**.

The **Mercado de Abastos** is a five-minute walk from the apartment and is, without exaggerating, my favorite place in town. It's not a tourist attraction: it's where local people shop, with the same familiar voices and fish that came ashore that very morning.

## First things first: when to go

The rule is simple — **in the morning and early**. The real activity is from nine to half past one, with the best stock in the first hours. From midday the stalls start closing and what's left is what nobody chose.

Saturdays are the big day: more stalls, more people, more atmosphere. Sundays, closed. And in August it's worth going even earlier, because the whole town has the same idea as you.

Neighbor's tip: **bring a cloth bag and some cash**. Many stalls already accept cards, but at the bars and small stalls cash is still king.

## The route: what I order in each area

**Fishmonger's.** It's the heart of the place. What I look for depending on the day:

- **Bluefin tuna** during almadraba season (spring). Ask for a loin, not fillets: you can slice it better yourself.
- **Anchovies and sardines** — incredibly cheap, and grilled at the apartment they're a ten-minute dinner.
- **Shrimp** if you intend to try making tortillitas.
- **Ortiguillas** (sea anemones), if you dare. Pure sea flavor, very Cádiz.
- **Choco** (cuttlefish) to cook on the grill with garlic and parsley. Foolproof.

And something outsiders don't know: **ask them to clean it for you**. They do it free of charge and it saves you half an hour of cooking and a complicated bin situation.

**Fruit and vegetable stall.** Local tomato in summer — ours looks nothing like supermarket ones — peppers, and in season, oranges and avocados from the region. Ask what's good this week: they'll tell you honestly, because they want you to come back tomorrow.

**Butcher's.** Here the magic word is **retinto**: the indigenous cattle breed of the area. A retinto sirloin or entrecôte on the grill with coarse salt and nothing else is one of those simple meals you remember.

**The bars.** Inside the market there are bars for breakfast. A coffee, a toast with manteca colorá or, if it's your lucky day, a **small shrimp omelette** freshly made. Having breakfast there standing up, surrounded by shopping carts, is the closest thing to being from Conil you can do in a week.

## Two recipes to make in the apartment

Our kitchen has everything — oven, induction, microwave, dishwasher — so make the most of it.

**1. Choco on the grill (15 minutes).**
Ask for it cleaned. Very hot pan, a drizzle of oil, five minutes each side. Off the heat: very finely chopped garlic, parsley, a squeeze of lemon. With bread and a cold beer you don't need anything else.

**2. Local tomato salad (5 minutes).**
Tomato from the market in season, sliced thick. Flaky salt, good olive oil, and that's it. No vinegar, no onion, no experiments. If the tomato is good, any addition ruins it.

And if you want the advanced level: ask for shrimp, mix them with chickpea flour, very cold water, spring onion and parsley, and fry thin spoonfuls in plenty of oil. The first ones will come out wrong. The fourth will come out okay. The sixth will be better than at many bars.

## Why I'm telling you this

Because the market is the difference between visiting Conil and **living Conil for a week**. Buying fish caught by your neighbor's neighbor, cooking it at home, eating in the living room with the window open… no restaurant can give you that.

And yes, it's also cheaper. Many families staying with us shop at the market twice a week and eat out for dinner two nights. It works out cheaper and you eat better.
