Traveling to the beach with kids isn't the same as traveling to the beach. I know because I see families arrive every summer with the car turned into storage and the same question on their faces: *"now what?"*

This is the guide I wish they'd given me when I started seeing nieces, nephews and friends with children arrive. Conil is a good town for families — quiet, flat, everything close by — but it has its tricks.

## The right beach by age

**Ages 0–3: early morning or last hour, and shade is sacred.**
At this age the plan is simple: sand to touch, calm water to dip feet in, and a proper nap. **Los Bateles**, our urban beach, works very well because you have the promenade with beach bars, showers, a lifeguard, and everything three minutes from the apartment — if you forget something, you go up and down in ten minutes, which is impossible at many beaches in the area. Avoid the central hours (11:00–16:00 in July–August): early morning, breakfast at the beach bar, beach, lunch and nap. That rhythm works for everyone.

**Ages 4–8: Bateles or Fontanilla, and pools in the coves.**
Now there's swimming and a desire for small waves. **La Fontanilla**, a walk north, is somewhat calmer than Bateles in August but with the same services. And here's my favorite trick: in the **calas de Roche**, as the tide goes out, **pools** form where little ones spend hours — literally hours — without anyone losing sight of them. Bring buckets; the prize is worth it.

**Ages 9–12: first adventures.**
This is the perfect age for **surf or paddle lessons** at Bateles or El Palmar — there are local schools with instructors used to kids — and for the walk to the lighthouse along the cliff, which at that age is already enjoyed like a little expedition.

## Routines that work (learned through trial and error)

- **Lunch and nap are untouchable.** The midday sun punishes; use it to eat peacefully and sleep. Vacations don't have to be a marathon.
- **The stroller holds up, the towel doesn't.** For coves, a baby carrier is better: the paths have steps.
- **Water and snacks always in the backpack.** At the calas de Roche there's no kiosk nearby; what you bring is what there is.
- **Scream and swimsuit.** The Costa de la Luz sun is deceptive because there's breeze; it burns just as much.
- **Early dinner every other day.** Many family-friendly places serve from eight without a problem; on the other days, the kids can watch the evening stroll with an ice cream while you have dinner later.

## More things to do with kids in town

- **Evening promenade:** flat path, atmosphere, ice cream shops. The universal plan.
- **Playgrounds** scattered around the old town and the promenade area; ask me which one suits which age group.
- **Mercado de Abastos:** kids usually enjoy it more than you'd expect — fruit stalls, fish still moving around, and a small shrimp omelette at the bar.
- **Strong wind days (Levante):** indoor plan — cinema in season, La Chanca museum, or a trip to Vejer (20 min) which even delights those who say white villages bore them.
