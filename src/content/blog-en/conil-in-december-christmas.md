In December Conil goes back to being a village. The seafront belongs to whoever is walking the dog, there is room at the bar and you park near home without circling the block. If you are coming for beach weather, fair warning: the water is cold. If you are coming for the real Conil, you picked the right month.

## The calendar at a glance

This is what repeats every December. The exact dates change, and this year's programme comes out at the end of November.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| End of November | Christmas lights switched on and the first zambombas |
| 5–8 December | December long weekend: market, nativity scene and the House of the Three Kings at Plaza Santa Catalina |
| Mid-December | Living nativity at La Chanca and Father Christmas parade through the centre |
| 20 December | Father Christmas tours El Colorado |
| 24 and 31 December | Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve at Plaza de España |
| 30 December | Children's new year's eve |
| 2 January | Royal Postman parade |
| 5 January | The Three Kings arrive along La Fontanilla beach, then the great parade |

## The long weekend: 5 to 8

In 2026 it runs from Saturday 5 to Tuesday 8. Those are the four liveliest days of the month: the village fills up, restaurants run at full tilt and the centre is full of lights.

Two tips if you come then: book a table in advance, and park at the Recinto Ferial or around the Mercado de Abastos, which is almost always free outside high season. You do not need a car in the centre — the [three-day itinerary without a car](/en/blog/conil-3-days-no-car/) is built for exactly that.

## The square is the centre of everything

Plaza Santa Catalina holds most of Christmas: the Christmas market, the municipal nativity scene and the House of the Three Kings, which open around the long weekend and stay open past King's Day.

Around the square there are zambombas almost every weekend — the ones run by the brotherhoods and local associations, with pestiñá pastries and hot chocolate included — plus a charity chocolate tasting organised by the town's cooks for the toy campaign.

The living nativity is the one children love most: in recent years it has been set up at the Centro Cultural La Chanca over one December weekend, with animals, artisans and scenes spread across the building.

## Father Christmas, chimes and the Kings

Father Christmas shows up twice: a parade through the centre in mid-December and another through the El Colorado district a few days later. For the little ones there is the children's new year's eve on 30 December, with chimes and fake snow.

On 2 January the Royal Postman comes to the square to collect the letters. And 5 January is the big day: in the morning the Three Kings arrive **along La Fontanilla beach** and the procession walks up into the centre; in the afternoon the great parade crosses the streets and ends at Santa Catalina.

## Where to check the programme

Each year's full programme is published at the end of November on the [Conil council website](https://www.conildelafrontera.es/navidad) and announced day by day on its Instagram, [@ayto_conil](https://www.instagram.com/ayto_conil/). The Fiestas department, which organises almost all of it, also posts on its [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/delegaciondefiestas.deconil/).

Two practical warnings: the council has no official TikTok account, and the tourism portal's events calendar is not always up to date. If you are coming for a specific event, confirm it on the council website or Instagram before planning the trip.

## What people eat in December

December is stew and seafood month. At the [Mercado de Abastos](/en/blog/conil-food-market-guide/), right opposite the apartment, you will find fish, shellfish and local vegetables: the simplest way to put together a good meal without booking anything.

The bars in the centre do the winter classics — stews, fish from the bay and the usual tapas. At home it is pestiños, mantecados and turrón, bought from the local bakery and gone before Christmas Eve.

## Plans that do not depend on the weather

- **Torre de Guzmán**: climb the tower for the view over the coast.
- **La Chanca**: cultural centre with a programme all month.
- **Old town**: wandering the empty streets under the lights is the best free plan of the month.
- **Hiking**: if it clears, the [Roche cliffs walk](/en/blog/hiking-roche-cliffs/) takes a morning.
- **Beach**: not for swimming, but for walking. Los Bateles and Castilnovo at low tide are something else.

## The practical bits

December is low season, and that shows in the good things: parking, prices and quiet. Also in others: most beach bars are closed, some restaurants take weekdays off and shops shorten their hours. Call ahead if you are coming for a specific place.
