In December Conil goes back to being a village. The seafront belongs to whoever is walking the dog, there is room at the bar and you can park near home without circling the block. If you are coming for beach weather, fair warning: the water is cold. If you are coming for the real Conil, you picked the right month.

## What the weather does

December here is mild rather than cold. At midday you can eat outside with a jacket on; at night it is properly cool. The levante wind shows up some days and rain arrives in short bursts: it can pour all morning and clear up by lunchtime.

Plan around that and everything works: a morning walk or a market run, a long lunch, and an afternoon of zambomba singing or a sofa.

## The December long weekend: 5 to 8

In 2026 the long weekend runs from Saturday 5 to Tuesday 8. Those are the four liveliest days of the month: the village fills up, restaurants run at full tilt and the old town is full of lights.

Two tips if you come then: book a table in advance, and park at the Recinto Ferial or around the Mercado de Abastos, which is almost always free outside high season. You do not need a car in the centre — the [three-day itinerary without a car](/en/blog/conil-3-days-no-car/) is built for exactly that.

## The village Christmas

The Christmas lights go on at the end of November, and from then on the calendar fills up. Plaza Santa Catalina is the centre of it all: the Christmas market, the municipal nativity scene and the House of the Three Kings are set up there.

Around the square there are zambombas almost every weekend — the traditional ones run by the brotherhoods and local associations, usually with pestiñá pastries and hot chocolate. The living nativity, held at the Centro Cultural La Chanca over one December weekend in recent years, is the one children love most: animals, artisans and scenes spread across the building.

Father Christmas also makes an appearance: a parade through the centre in mid-December and another through the El Colorado district a few days later. For the little ones there is the children's new year's eve on 30 December, chimes and fake snow included.

## The finale: the Royal Postman and the Kings by the sea

On 2 January the Royal Postman comes to the square to collect the children's letters. And 5 January is the big day: in the morning the Three Kings arrive **along La Fontanilla beach** and the procession walks up into the centre; in the afternoon the great parade crosses the streets and ends at Santa Catalina.

It is a scene you do not see in many places: Melchor, Gaspar and Baltasar coming in along the shoreline while the town waits on the promenade.

One practical note: exact dates and times change every year. The full programme is published on the [Conil council website](https://www.conildelafrontera.es/navidad) and its social channels. If you are planning a trip around a specific event, confirm it there first.

## What people eat in December

December is stew and seafood month. At the [Mercado de Abastos](/en/blog/conil-food-market-guide/), right opposite the apartment, you will find fish, shellfish and local vegetables: the simplest way to put together a good meal without booking anything.

The bars in the centre do the winter classics — stews, fish from the bay and the usual tapas. At home it is pestiños, mantecados and turrón, bought from the local bakery and gone well before Christmas Eve.

If you plan to cook, the apartment has a full kitchen and the market is a step away. That combination works especially well in December: shop mid-morning, eat slowly.

## Plans that do not depend on the weather

- **Torre de Guzmán**: climb the tower for the view over the coast.
- **La Chanca**: cultural centre with a programme all month.
- **Old town**: wandering the empty streets under the lights is the best free plan of the month.
- **Hiking**: if it clears, the [Roche cliffs walk](/en/blog/hiking-roche-cliffs/) takes a morning and the wind is welcome.
- **Beach**: not for swimming, but for walking. Los Bateles and Castilnovo at low tide are something else.

## The practical bits

December is low season, and that shows in the good things: parking, prices and quiet. Also in others: most beach bars are closed, some restaurants take weekdays off and shops in the centre shorten their hours.

Call ahead if you are coming for a specific place, and if your plan is the long weekend or King's Day, move early — that is when the village is busiest all month.
