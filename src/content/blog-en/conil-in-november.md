Let me say something that sounds odd coming from someone who rents an apartment on the beach: **my favorite month in Conil is November**.

It's not posturing. It's that in November something happens that's impossible in August: the village becomes the village again.

## The numbers first

In July and August Conil goes from its 24,000 registered residents to around **160,000 people**. In November we're ourselves again. That translates into very concrete things:

- **Accommodation prices** that drop dramatically from the August peak.
- **Park you wherever you want.** The national sport of August disappears.
- **Table without reservation** at almost any place, at almost any time.
- **Empty beaches.** Fourteen kilometres of coast for very few people.

## The real weather (without selling smoke)

November on the Costa de la Luz isn't summer and I'm not going to paint it that way. The honest version: **mild highs that often hover around twenty degrees**, cool mornings, and a real chance of rain — it's one of the wettest months of the year here.

What you do have almost guaranteed are **days of clean light** when walking along the cliff in a long-sleeved shirt is a pleasure. Swimming is for the brave; walking, for everyone.

My advice: come with a light jacket, a small umbrella and zero expectations of a tan. What you take away from here in November is something else.

## What's open and what's closed

This is the part nobody tells you, so here it goes honestly:

- **The old quarter bars and restaurants** — open, all of them. The ones that close in winter are the beach chiringuitos and some of the more seasonal places on the promenade.
- **The Mercado de Abastos** — open, vital, and in November you have it almost to yourself.
- **Surf schools** — closed, but the surfers are in the water. November swells are some of the best of the year.
- **Buses** — reduced timetable. Check times before relying on them.

## Who November is for

- **Couples** who want Conil without the August crowds — this is your month.
- **Remote workers** — the village has fibre, cafés with good coffee and the slow-living atmosphere that's gold if you can work from anywhere.
- **Hikers and cyclists** — the Roche cliff, the countryside, the green route to Vejer...
- **Gastronomy lovers** — no queues, no reservations needed, and the market without the August frenzy.

## A honest warning

It's not the month for swimming every day, non-stop beach or summer nightlife. If that's what you're after, come in June or September.

But if you want the Conil that Conileños keep for ourselves, November delivers.
