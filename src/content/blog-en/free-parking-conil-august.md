Let's talk about the touchy subject of every August: **parking**.

First, context without decoration. Conil has around 24,000 registered residents. In August we reach roughly **160,000 people** between town and beach. Do the quick maths and two things make sense: why there's so much life here… and why finding a space at two in the afternoon is the national sport.

## The golden rule: whoever arrives before 11:00 wins

It's not an exotic trick, it's pure maths. Most beachgoers make two moves: one around half twelve (lunch) and one around six (second swim). There's rotation in between, but the good spots — shade, close to the promenade — are gone before midday.

If you arrive **before 11:00**, even on the fifteenth of August, you'll find a space without drama. If you arrive at 13:30 hunting for a spot on the seafront, prepare for a twenty-minute lap and a mediocre mood.

## The zones I consider (closest to easiest)

I'll describe areas rather than exact addresses, because specific streets change regulation year to year and I don't want to hand you stale information:

**1. The seafront promenade and around it.** It's what everyone tries first and therefore the first to fill. In summer much of it is paid parking during daytime hours — read the meter and the sign carefully, because enforcement and tow trucks are serious business in July and August.

**2. Northern entrance, park-and-walk style.** When I'm getting out of the car planning to stay all day, I often park along the northern entrance and walk five to ten minutes. You gain two things: zero stress on the way back and zero parking tickets. Conil is small; from there you're at the beach in a stroll.

**3. Around Los Bateles and the Río Salado.** The area towards the river has more options depending on the day and hour, but it's also the most closely watched, so apply the golden rule with even more reason. And a neighbour's warning: **never leave the car in the beach access paths, on the sand or in the riverbed** even if you see others doing it. The tow truck works fast and with enthusiasm there.

## The mistakes that end in a fine (I see them every August)

- **Mistaking a blue line for free parking.** Extended hours in summer. Photograph the sign, always.
- **Two wheels on the pavement** "just for a second". Guaranteed fine.
- **Parking in beach access paths** or loading zones. No warning here: straight to the tow truck.
- **Leaving the car for three days** in the same rotating-zone space without feeding the meter. Local officers know the regular cars; the stationary visitor stands out.

## The alternative nobody considers: come without a car

Now, the advice that genuinely changes your holiday: **Conil is best enjoyed on foot**.

From the centre — and from our apartment, which is in the old quarter — the beach is three minutes away, the market five and the lighthouse a quarter-hour stroll. For trips to El Palmar or Vejer there are taxis and buses, and day to day you won't touch your keys once.

Plenty of guests arrived this year obsessed with parking and ended up using the car only on arrival and departure day. That's the master move: **park once, well away from the bustle, and forget about it**.

## Summary for the fridge door

1. Arrive **before 11:00** or after 18:00.
2. Blue zone = read the sign; in August there's no grace period.
3. Northern entrance and park-and-walk: five minutes on foot saves twenty of circling.
4. Beach accesses, riverbeds and loading bays: absolutely not.
5. The premium option is not using the car at all.
